01 / OVERVIEW

Human judgment meets generative detail.

Diffusion-based super-resolution can create visually plausible detail that is not supported by the low-resolution input. Do image quality metrics agree with people?

We introduce DISRQAD, a subjective-quality dataset and diagnostic benchmark with mean opinion scores for 14,000 outputs from ten diffusion and four non-diffusion methods, four low-resolution degradation conditions, and ×2 / ×4 upscaling. We evaluate 51 standard full-reference and no-reference configurations and 11 adapted variants.

Agreement with MOS is substantially weaker on diffusion outputs. The strongest standard NR baseline reaches 0.431 SRCC on diffusion SR; the strongest standard NR correlation on non-diffusion SR is 0.813. A pruned and distilled Q-ReAlign-mini student reaches 0.496 on diffusion SR.

What the scores mean. DISRQAD measures perceived output quality. Raters see neither the LR input nor the HR reference; visually attractive but scene-inconsistent details may receive high scores. Fidelity and semantic preservation are separate assessment targets.

Research context: fidelity, appearance and related benchmarks

Super-resolution can introduce oversharpening, ringing, unnatural textures, hallucinated objects and changes to salient regions such as faces. A visually attractive output may differ from the LR evidence and the HR reference. DISRQAD isolates the perceived quality of the output as one assessment target.

Full-reference (FR) metrics compare an output to HR: PSNR and SSIM measure pixel or structural correspondence; LPIPS and DISTS compare features; ERQA emphasizes edges; A-FINE relaxes the perfect-reference assumption. No-reference (NR) metrics see only the output: NIQE uses natural-scene statistics; MUSIQ learns subjective ratings; Q-Align and DEQA use vision–language representations. NeuralSBS learns SR preferences; FGResQ combines scores and restoration rankings. Reduced-reference methods use limited reference information and are not a separate track in this benchmark.

SBS180K records preferences between outputs from the same input. FGRestore and LL-Bench combine pairwise preferences and image-level scores across restoration tasks. The RQI study examines FR / NR disagreement on SR preferences. DISRQAD keeps the task fixed to still-image SR and varies generator family, using direct five-category ratings. Video-SR benchmarks such as VSRQAD and studies of codec-compressed or streaming SR address temporal and video-specific artifacts outside this setting.

SRCC measures ranking agreement; PLCC measures linear agreement. Kendall correlation is another common IQA measure, but the supplied numeric tables here report SRCC and PLCC. Bibliographic references for the methods and related work are available in the full PDF.

Generator matters

PassionSR and ResShift yield the highest within-method SRCC for 36 and 20 of 62 variants. DiT4SR yields the lowest for 38. CODSR and PASD have median SRCCs of 0.135 and 0.158.

Blur is often easier to rank

On diffusion SR, blur yields the highest SRCC for almost all metrics. The distilled student reaches 0.542 on blur, compared with 0.466 on resized inputs.

Scale changes the picture

52 of 62 variants have higher diffusion SRCC at ×4 than ×2. For non-diffusion SR, only 25 variants follow this direction.

05 / DOMAIN ADAPTATION

Fine-tuning helps. Transfer is uneven.

SR-specific supervision can improve diffusion agreement while reducing performance elsewhere.

Ten baselines were fine-tuned on SRIQA-Bench: AHIQ, LPIPS-AlexNet, LPIPS-VGG, PieAPP, TOPIQ-FR, ARNIQA, MUSIQ, LIQE, TOPIQ-NR and Q-ReAlign-mini. Three improved on both SR tracks; other effects depend on the metric and domain.

Simple fine-tuning substantially reduced quality on non-SR distortions in KonIQ-10K, KADID and SPAQ. Specialized training should preserve broader IQA behavior rather than optimize only one track.

07 / SUPPLEMENTARY

The details behind the benchmark.

Construction choices, annotation evidence, training settings and limitations from Appendices A–D.

A Dataset construction & source selection

More than 468,000 candidates from KonIQ-10K, COCO (train / test / validation), AVA and FLIVE are represented by SI, blockiness, SAM object count, Q-Align, ARNIQA embeddings and CLIP embeddings. K-means forms 20,000 clusters using blockiness, SAM, ARNIQA and CLIP; a medoid represents each cluster.

The intersection of high-SI and high-Q-Align candidates is selected via quantile binning toward a target of 10,000 sources. The final selection contains 10,036 images with thresholds SI = 302.53006 and Q-Align = 3.9648438. Q-Align is used before SR generation to filter source quality.

Controlled LR generation

Bicubic ×2 and ×4 downscaling, with resized-only inputs or single degradations: Gaussian blur (7 × 7 kernel, σ = 1.2), Gaussian noise (σ = 8), or JPEG quality 50. Eight LR images per source yield 80,288 inputs.

Output selection

If a method lacks the target scale, its output is generated at the smallest available scale above the target and resized back with bicubic interpolation. All ten diffusion methods yield 802,880 outputs; four classic methods yield 321,152.

SR-grounding segmentation provides distortion class areas and CW-SSIM features between the maps and SR images. Q-Align and TOPIQ-FR add complementary quality features. Separate K-means selections yield 10,000 diffusion and 4,000 non-diffusion medoids. Semantic-cluster coverage after this output selection has not been established.

B Subjective protocol, pilot & quality control

Pairwise preference

Choose the better output from the same reference. Bradley–Terry aggregation provides a pilot baseline. Exhaustive cost grows quadratically; independently fitted BT scores do not share a scale across references.

Full-reference rating

See HR and SR together. Categories: very poor compared with reference, significantly worse, slightly worse, comparable, better.

No-reference rating

See only the SR output. Categories: very poor, poor, satisfactory, good, excellent. Direct MOS shares a scale across images; cost grows linearly.

A 50-output pilot

Five SR outputs for each of ten references span different distortions and quality levels. Each MOS protocol collects at least 20 judgments per image; pairwise evaluation collects at least 20 per pair. Pairwise tasks use Subjectify.us; MOS tasks use Yandex Tasks.

Training and control items

Nine experts first rate 20 images in-lab. The five images with the highest variance are excluded; the remaining 15 prepare training examples and control items. Assessors must complete training and answer control items reliably.

Why select no-reference MOS?

For each reference and each protocol, a nonlinear mapping ai xbi + ci is fitted to Bradley–Terry scores with Levenberg–Marquardt, minimizing squared residuals. NR MOS has closer fitted agreement in this pilot. The small sample does not establish general superiority of NR ratings.

Choosing the annotation budget

Thirteen ratings per image give pilot SROCC 0.983084 against a 20-rating baseline, with 95% CI [0.970229, 0.990415]. This is within-pilot agreement, not uncertainty for each final MOS. The final protocol retains training and controls and collects at least 13 NR ratings per image.

C Student architecture, losses & training recipe

Two complementary prediction heads

The Qwen3.5-VL language stack comprises six groups of three Gated DeltaNet modules and one full-attention module. Pruning leaves one Gated DeltaNet and one full-attention module per group. The vocabulary is restricted to 30 tokens.

A token head scores five ordered quality labels. A regression head applies a 1024 → 256 projection, GELU, dropout, 256 → 1 projection and sigmoid to the last hidden state. The final estimate is ŷ = αT + (1 − α)M, with α learned jointly.

Stage 1 · hierarchical distillation

The unpruned Q-ReAlign-mini teacher supervises every third visual layer, corresponding language layers, full-attention maps, scores and five-token distributions. The student also learns MOS on KonIQ-10K, KADID-10K and SPAQ using Q-Align's splits.

Stage 2 · SR adaptation

Remove the teacher; combine 990 SRIQA-Bench images with 248 general-IQA replay images. The 1,100 rated SRIQA-Bench images include HR and SR images and are split at the individual-image level. Retain the first four label losses and add L2 regularization toward the stage-1 checkpoint with weight 10⁻⁴. Train for two epochs, effective batch 8 (physical 4); the other 110 images select the checkpoint.

Both stages use AdamW, weight decay 0.01, 5% linear warm-up followed by cosine decay. Learning rates: language 2 × 10⁻⁵, vision 2 × 10⁻⁶, heads 10⁻⁴.

Further diffusion-domain adaptation

Starting from the SRIQA-Bench-adapted checkpoint, further fine-tune on the open part of DISRQAD using the same strategy, then evaluate on the private held-out portion.

D Limitations & interpretation

DISRQAD targets perceived output quality under a no-reference protocol. It does not measure faithfulness to the LR evidence or semantic preservation. Sources favor detailed, high-quality images; LR inputs use controlled, single degradations. Compound real-world degradations require separate evaluation.

Output clustering does not establish semantic coverage after selection. The small protocol pilot does not demonstrate general equivalence between rating procedures; its confidence intervals do not quantify each final MOS. SRIQA-Bench checkpoint-selection results are validation results.
