01 / OVERVIEW

Human judgment meets generative detail.

Diffusion-based super-resolution can create visually plausible detail that is not supported by the low-resolution input. Do image quality metrics agree with people?

We introduce DISRQAD, a subjective-quality dataset and diagnostic benchmark with mean opinion scores for 14,000 outputs from ten diffusion and four non-diffusion methods, four low-resolution degradation conditions, and ×2 / ×4 upscaling. We evaluate 51 standard full-reference and no-reference configurations and 11 adapted variants.

Agreement with MOS is substantially weaker on diffusion outputs. The strongest standard NR baseline reaches 0.431 SRCC on diffusion SR; the strongest standard NR correlation on non-diffusion SR is 0.813. A pruned and distilled Q-ReAlign-mini student reaches 0.496 on diffusion SR.

What the scores mean. DISRQAD measures perceived output quality. Raters see neither the LR input nor the HR reference; visually attractive but scene-inconsistent details may receive high scores. Fidelity and semantic preservation are separate assessment targets.

Research context: fidelity, appearance and related benchmarks

Super-resolution can introduce oversharpening, ringing, unnatural textures, hallucinated objects and changes to salient regions such as faces. A visually attractive output may differ from the LR evidence and the HR reference. DISRQAD isolates the perceived quality of the output as one assessment target.

Full-reference (FR) metrics compare an output to HR: PSNR and SSIM measure pixel or structural correspondence; LPIPS and DISTS compare features; ERQA emphasizes edges; A-FINE relaxes the perfect-reference assumption. No-reference (NR) metrics see only the output: NIQE uses natural-scene statistics; MUSIQ learns subjective ratings; Q-Align and DEQA use vision–language representations. NeuralSBS learns SR preferences; FGResQ combines scores and restoration rankings. Reduced-reference methods use limited reference information and are not a separate track in this benchmark.

SBS180K records preferences between outputs from the same input. FGRestore and LL-Bench combine pairwise preferences and image-level scores across restoration tasks. The RQI study examines FR / NR disagreement on SR preferences. DISRQAD keeps the task fixed to still-image SR and varies generator family, using direct five-category ratings. Video-SR benchmarks such as VSRQAD and studies of codec-compressed or streaming SR address temporal and video-specific artifacts outside this setting.

SRCC measures ranking agreement; PLCC measures linear agreement. Kendall correlation is another common IQA measure, but the supplied numeric tables here report SRCC and PLCC. Bibliographic references for the methods and related work are available in the full PDF.

How does DISRQAD compare with existing SR datasets?

Earlier datasets emphasize non-diffusion SR. SRIQA-Bench and ISRGen-QA include five diffusion methods each. DISRQAD expands generator coverage within a common direct-rating protocol. Pairwise preferences and absolute scores answer different questions.

Generator matters

PassionSR and ResShift yield the highest within-method SRCC for 36 and 20 of 62 variants. DiT4SR yields the lowest for 38. CODSR and PASD have median SRCCs of 0.135 and 0.158.

Blur is often easier to rank

On diffusion SR, blur yields the highest SRCC for almost all metrics. The distilled student reaches 0.542 on blur, compared with 0.466 on resized inputs.

Scale changes the picture

52 of 62 variants have higher diffusion SRCC at ×4 than ×2. For non-diffusion SR, only 25 variants follow this direction.

05 / DOMAIN ADAPTATION

Fine-tuning helps. Transfer is uneven.

SR-specific supervision can improve diffusion agreement while reducing performance elsewhere.

Ten baselines were fine-tuned on SRIQA-Bench: AHIQ, LPIPS-AlexNet, LPIPS-VGG, PieAPP, TOPIQ-FR, ARNIQA, MUSIQ, LIQE, TOPIQ-NR and Q-ReAlign-mini. Three improved on both SR tracks; other effects depend on the metric and domain.

Simple fine-tuning substantially reduced quality on non-SR distortions in KonIQ-10K, KADID and SPAQ. Specialized training should preserve broader IQA behavior rather than optimize only one track.

Baseline fine-tuning settings

Batch size 8, up to 30 epochs, initial learning rate 10⁻⁴, MSE against MOS, Adam optimizer, and checkpoint selection by SRCC using the IQA-PyTorch interface. LIQE uses 5 × 10⁻⁶ for convergence; Q-ReAlign-mini is limited to 2 epochs because of its model size and rapid convergence.
